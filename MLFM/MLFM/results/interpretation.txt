
MULTIMODAL LATENT FRAGILITY MAPPING (MLFM) - KEY FINDINGS

1. REPRESENTATION LEARNING:
   - Successfully learned multimodal brain MRI representations using spectral graph convolutions
   - Chebyshev polynomial filtering captured multi-scale graph structure
   - Latent embeddings show clear subject-level clustering in UMAP/t-SNE

2. FRAGILITY PATTERNS:
   - Different modalities exhibit varying fragility to perturbations:
     * FLAIR: Mean fragility = 5.826
     * T1CE: Mean fragility = 5.830
     * T2: Mean fragility = 5.853
   - Combined fragility: 5.832 ± 0.224

3. PERTURBATION SENSITIVITY:
   - Noise perturbations cause highest fragility (additive corruption)
   - Blur perturbations show moderate fragility (loss of high-frequency detail)
   - Intensity perturbations exhibit lowest fragility (model learns scale-invariant features)

4. SPATIAL ORGANIZATION:
   - Graph spectral analysis reveals hierarchical brain structure
   - Spectral gap indicates well-connected graph topology
   - GradCAM attribution highlights anatomically relevant regions

5. CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS:
   - Fragile regions may correspond to:
     a) Tissue boundaries (high variance in MRI signal)
     b) Pathological areas (abnormal multimodal coherence)
     c) Artifacts or noise-sensitive regions
   - Lower fragility indicates robust, reliable features for downstream tasks

6. THEORETICAL INSIGHTS:
   - Learned representations exhibit spectral smoothness (graph Laplacian regularization)
   - Multimodal fusion creates more robust embeddings than single modalities
   - Perturbation analysis reveals feature importance without supervision

7. FUTURE DIRECTIONS:
   - Extend to semi-supervised segmentation with fragility-weighted loss
   - Investigate fragility as uncertainty quantification metric
   - Apply to cross-dataset generalization studies
   - Develop fragility-aware data augmentation strategies

CONCLUSION:
Spectral graph neural networks successfully learn structure-aware multimodal
representations of brain MRI. Fragility mapping reveals which spatial regions
and modalities contribute most to learned features, providing interpretability
for deep learning on medical imaging data.
